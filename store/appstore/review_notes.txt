This app is intended for unlisted distribution.

HJViewer is a remote desktop app for Yonsei University faculty and staff: the iPhone or iPad views and controls a Windows PC that runs HJViewer for Windows, and is never viewed or controlled itself. It works only with the HJViewer server that Project TF, a team at Yonsei University, runs. It reaches PCs behind firewalls over HTTPS (port 443) and has its own account service (a university login, the user's own PCs, password-less connections to them, waking a PC through a relay device on its network). It is based on the open-source RustDesk (AGPL-3.0) and is not affiliated with it. It costs nothing, has no ads or in-app purchases, and works the same in every region.

External services: the server checks portal logins with the Yonsei University SSO; public STUN servers help devices connect directly. The app supports iPhone and iPad with iOS 13.0 or later.

This server is for Yonsei University faculty and staff, so sign in first with the demo account (Settings > Account > Log in). Login uses their university portal accounts; the app has no sign-up. Without a login, an app connects only with the operator's access password, and a PC accepts connections only with it or with a one-time support code from a staff member. The demo account needs neither.

The demo account reaches only the demo Windows PC: ID {{DEMO_PC_ID}}, password {{DEMO_PC_PASSWORD}}. Type the Sign-In Information exactly (the user name goes in the "Yonsei portal ID" field): wrong passwords lock the account for up to a day. Settings shows it as "App Review"; its "My PCs" tab stays empty. If the app says "A new device logged in", it is another review device: tap "It is mine". Do the same on "Please check a device logged in to your account" and "Please check a device that was logged in". To delete the account, open Settings > Account > Delete account; signing in again afterwards starts an empty account.

Account types: staff use Yonsei portal accounts, and administrators are staff accounts that the operator names on the server. Both are real people's accounts, so App Review gets this demo account, which lacks the staff features below.

The screenshots of the My PCs list and of the offer to wake a PC that is off show a staff account with PCs in its list and a relay device on their network; the demo account cannot show them.

Staff accounts see "Create support code" under Settings > Account and on the My PCs tab: a one-time code, sent with a link to HJViewer for Windows, that lets the PC of someone without a login accept connections for two hours; when the staff member's device connects, that PC's connection window shows a signed "Yonsei faculty or staff (verified login)" mark. Administrators also see a read-only "Admin view: users and PCs" under Settings > Account.

The app also has text chat with the person at the PC in a session (end-to-end encrypted, not stored on the server), session recording to the device, and a PC's camera view and terminal from its menu.

Face ID: only for an optional app lock (Settings > Security > App lock, off by default), which asks for Face ID or the passcode when the app opens or returns after a set time (1 minute by default) and covers its screens until then and in the background, so the app switcher shows no remote screen. The app gets only the result and sends nothing about it.

Local network: HJViewer asks for the local network only to connect directly (peer to peer) to a PC on the same network.

To try remote control: sign in, type the demo PC's ID in the "Remote ID" box on the Connection tab, tap the arrow and enter the PC's password.

Export compliance: the app's encryption (TLS, DTLS in WebRTC connections, and libsodium) uses standard algorithms only. Its Info.plist sets ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption to NO.
