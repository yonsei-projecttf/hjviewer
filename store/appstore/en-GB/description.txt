HJViewer is a remote desktop app: see and control a Windows PC from your iPhone or iPad. It is made by Project TF (프로젝트TF) at Yonsei University, and finds PCs through the HJViewer server that Project TF runs.

Reach PCs behind firewalls
The PC and the app make only outgoing connections, so a PC behind a firewall or router (NAT) is reached over HTTPS on port 443: peer to peer where the network allows, else through the server's encrypted relay.

Connect with an ID and a password
• Type the ID shown in HJViewer on the PC, then the PC's password. Log in first (below).
• Control the PC in mouse or touch mode; type Korean text and shortcuts on the on-screen keyboard.
• Send files between the PC and your iPhone or iPad.
• Recent PCs and your favourites appear as cards on the home screen.

Log in with a Yonsei portal account (faculty and staff)
The server is for Yonsei University faculty and staff: log in to connect and to use the features below. Without a login, only an access password from the operator lets you connect.
• My PCs: your Windows PCs with HJViewer installed and logged in to the same account appear in a list; pick one to connect.
• Password-less connections: when the operator turns this on, you connect to the PCs in your list without passwords. Their connection window shows a signed "Same account, verified" mark, and a PC locked at the start locks again at the end.
• Wake up: with a relay device the operator approved on your PC's network, the app can send that PC a wake signal, which can wake a wired PC on mains power with Wake-on-LAN on from sleep or shutdown, depending on its network card and BIOS.
• Support codes and links: a one-time code, sent with a link to HJViewer for Windows, lets the PC of someone without a login accept connections for two hours; when the device that made the code connects, that PC's connection window shows a signed "Yonsei faculty or staff (verified login)" mark.
• New login alerts: when another device logs in to your account, your other devices alert you when the app next opens. If it was not you, log that device out at once; its open password-less connections end within minutes (PCs with HJViewer 1.9.0 or later that reach the server).
• Delete account: Settings > Account > Delete account deletes your account at once. Your PCs leave My PCs, every device logged in to the account is logged out (others when they next reach the server), and the device list, relay approvals and wake records go too.
Only Yonsei University faculty and staff can log in; the app has no sign-up.

Security
• The screen, input, clipboard and files of a remote session are end-to-end encrypted; a relaying server passes on only data it cannot read.
• The app seals your portal password with the server's key; the server has it checked by the Yonsei SSO and forgets it at once. It is never stored.
• Each logged-in device has its own account key in its keychain, never synced or carried off by a backup; it signs every account request.
• App lock (optional, in Security settings): the app asks for Face ID, Touch ID or the passcode when it opens or you come back after a set time (1 minute by default), and covers its screens until then and in the background. iOS checks the biometrics; the app never gets them.
• There are no analytics, ads or crash reports.

Your iPhone or iPad is only the controller
Your iPhone or iPad cannot itself be viewed or controlled; the app never shares its screen.

Requirements
• An iPhone or iPad with iOS 13.0 or later
• The PC you connect to: Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit) running HJViewer for Windows, which Project TF provides
• To connect when nobody is at the PC, install HJViewer on it, log in on it, and set a permanent password. PCs that are asleep or off cannot be reached until woken.

Open source
HJViewer is based on the open-source RustDesk (AGPL-3.0) and is not affiliated with the RustDesk team. HJViewer is AGPL-3.0 too; its source code is on GitHub: https://github.com/yonsei-projecttf/hjviewer
